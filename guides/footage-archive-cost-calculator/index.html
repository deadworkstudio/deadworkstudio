    How this is worked out

      Paying to store footage you never use?

      Some of an archive is good stock footage. Stock Shredder finds the usable shots in raw footage, trims and grades them, and exports each one as its own clip with a title and keywords. Try it on 100 clips free.

      The three options, honestly.

      • Hard drives. Cheapest to start and easy to plug in. They wear out, so the real cost includes buying them again and copying everything across.
      • LTO tape. A big first cost for the drive, then cheap cartridges that hold a lot. The catch is generations: the LTO Program says LTO-10 drives don't read older tapes, so moving up means copying across.
      • Cloud. Nothing to buy and it's off site. You pay every month for as long as the footage is there, and the bill grows with the archive.

      FAQ

      Is LTO cheaper than hard drives?

      Usually over enough years and enough terabytes. The tape drive is a big first cost, but each cartridge holds a lot for little money. Put in your own prices: the break-even point depends on how much footage you keep.

      How much does cloud storage cost for footage?

      Backblaze B2 lists $6.95 per TB a month and Wasabi lists $7.99 per TB a month (November 2026). Ten TB for ten years is around $8,000 to $10,000 at those prices, before any growth.

      Can an LTO-10 drive read my LTO-9 tapes?

      No. The LTO Program says LTO-10 drives don't read or write older generations, so moving to LTO-10 means copying your tapes across while you still have an LTO-9 drive.

      How many copies should I keep?

      Many studios keep at least two, in different places. The calculator multiplies drives and tape by the copies you enter. Cloud is counted as one copy.

      Does this include my time?

      No. Copying to new drives or tapes takes days for a large archive. The calculator counts what you pay, not the hours.
