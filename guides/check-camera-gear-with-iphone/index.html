How to Use Your iPhone to Check Camera Gear

Three ways to check a packed case with the phone already in your pocket, from a Notes checklist to pointing the camera at the case.

The short version

  1. Notes checklist: one list per case, ticked when the case leaves and again at wrap. Free and quick to set up.
  2. A photo of the open case: a dated record of what was packed. Good backup, slow to check.
  3. An app that uses the camera: pan across the open case and see what's missing. This is what Loadout does.

Whichever you pick, start from a good list. Our camera gear checklist for filmmakers has one by kit.

Why the phone is the right tool.

Paper lists stay in the truck, and memory runs out at the end of a long day. The phone is in your pocket at the two moments that matter: when the case leaves and when it closes at wrap. It also adds the date and time to anything you save, which a paper list never does.

The question is how much the phone does for you. Here are three ways, from simplest to most automatic.

1. A checklist in Notes.

The Notes app on iPhone has checklists built in. Make one note per case, not one long list for the whole kit, so each check takes a minute.

  1. Name the note after the case. "A-cam case," "Lens case," "Power bag."
  2. One line per item. Number look-alikes: Battery 1, Battery 2, Card A, Card B.
  3. Tick it with the case open. Look at the item, then tick it. Don't tick from memory.
  4. Untick for the next check. Clear the ticks before the case leaves again.

Catches: anything you look for. Misses: anything you tick without looking, and there's no record of when the check happened. Our free checklist maker builds the list for your shoot type, and you can paste it into a note.

2. A photo of the open case.

Take one photo of each case open and fully packed, from above, before it closes. Your photo library keeps the date and time.

  • Good for: proving what was in a case on a given day, and spotting an empty slot when you compare two photos.
  • Hard part: nothing tells you what's missing. You have to compare the photo with a list yourself, and small items hide under cables.
  • Tip: photograph the case in the same position each time, so an empty slot stands out.

Pair it with the Notes list: tick the list, then take the photo as the record.

3. Let the camera check the case.

An app can do the comparison for you. Loadout is our iPhone app for this. You save each case as a kit, then point the camera at the open case.

  1. Build a kit once. Add each item in the case with the camera. Loadout uses that to recognize it by sight later. No stickers or QR codes.
  2. Open the case and pan. Items the camera recognizes are marked Seen. Anything it hasn't seen stays on the missing list.
  3. Tap to confirm. Seen is not packed. Look-alikes, items under other gear and dark cases wait for your tap, and only confirmed items count.
  4. All accounted for. When every item is confirmed, the check is saved with the date and time.
  • Works offline. Scanning and checking run on the iPhone, so no signal is needed.
  • Low light. Dark cases are harder to see. Loadout tells you when it's too dark and offers a one-tap light. Anything it still can't see, you check by hand.
  • At wrap. Wrap Day puts every kit into one check, and a reminder can go off when your phone leaves the location. You turn that on in Settings.
  • Limits. iPhone only for now (iOS 17 or later), with Android coming soon. Each iPhone keeps its own kits; sharing kits isn't in the app.

Loadout is free for 14 days, with no card needed. Most other iPhone gear apps keep lists you tick by hand; our comparison of camera gear inventory apps covers them.

FAQ

Can I check camera gear with my iPhone?

Yes. The simplest way is a checklist per case in the Notes app. A photo of each open case adds a record you can look back at. An app like Loadout uses the iPhone camera to check the open case against a saved kit.

Is there a camera gear checklist app for iPhone?

Several. Some are lists you tick by hand, like ShootPrep, PhotoPack Pro and GigBag. Loadout checks the open case with the camera instead. Our comparison of camera gear inventory apps covers what each one does.

Does checking gear with the iPhone camera need internet?

Not with Loadout. Scanning and checking run on the phone, so it works on stages, in basements and on remote locations.

What if two items look the same, like batteries?

Loadout doesn't guess. Identical items show as Seen, and each one needs your tap before it counts as packed. Numbering look-alikes on your list helps with any method.

Does it work on Android?

Loadout is iPhone only for now, on iOS 17 or later. Android is coming soon. The Notes and photo methods work on any phone.
