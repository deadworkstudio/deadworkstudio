Sony FX3 and FX6 Footage to Stock Clips, Fast

The short version

  1. Grade S-Log3 to a light Rec 709. No flat log uploads.
  2. Cut 10 to 20 second clips, one shot each.
  3. Keep native 4K and the frame rate you shot.
  4. Slow motion: use S&Q so the file is already slowed down.
  5. Export ProRes 422 HQ, audio off.

Stock Shredder does it in one pass. Our test: 1,000 graded 4K clips from FX6 and FX3 footage in 3 hours 1 minute.

Great footage, stuck on the card.

The FX3 and FX6 make it easy to come home with hours of clean 4K. Getting it to a stock site is the slow part: scrub, trim, grade S-Log3, export, name and keyword, clip after clip.

At about 2 minutes a clip, a 500-clip shoot is 16+ hours at the desk. Most of it is the same steps again and again.

Sony footage, stock site rules.

  • S-Log3 needs a grade. Adobe Stock won't take uncorrected log, and Shutterstock prefers Rec 709. Keep it light. Buyers grade it again.
  • Native 4K is fine. Don't upscale, and don't downscale unless a site asks.
  • Frame rates. 23.98, 24, 25, 29.97, 30, 50, 59.94 and 60 work everywhere. 48 works on Pond5 and Shutterstock, not Adobe Stock.
  • Slow motion. S&Q mode records slow motion at a normal playback rate, which is what sites want.
  • Audio off. The built-in mic picks up handling noise. Strip it unless the sound is the point.
  • File size. Adobe Stock caps files at 3.9 GB. Short ProRes clips stay well under.

Sources: Adobe Stock technical requirements, Shutterstock video requirements, Pond5 footage guidelines. Checked November 2026. Full comparison in How to Prepare Footage for Adobe Stock, Pond5 and Shutterstock.

The by-hand way.

  1. Import the XAVC files into Premiere, Resolve or Final Cut.
  2. Scrub and set an in and out for each shot.
  3. Grade S-Log3 to Rec 709 with a LUT and a touch of contrast.
  4. Export each shot as its own ProRes file.
  5. Name the file and its folder.
  6. Write a title and keywords.

Our average is about 2 minutes per clip, every clip.

What to know before you start.

Stock Shredder handles the repetitive parts. A few things stay with you.

  • You review. It finds the usable shots. Your eye picks the ones that sell.
  • You set the look. Save your favorite S-Log3 grade once and it applies in one click.
  • You upload. Names and keywords are ready to paste into each site.
  • It exports ProRes. Every site takes it, so one export covers them all.

When by hand still makes sense.

  • A few hero shots that need a custom grade.
  • Clips built from several shots, like a speed ramp.

For a full card, every clip needs the same steps. That's the part worth handing off.

FAQ

Can I upload S-Log3 footage to stock sites?

Grade it first. Adobe Stock asks contributors not to send uncorrected log, and Shutterstock prefers Rec 709. A light grade from S-Log3 to Rec 709 is what buyers want.

What export format is best for Sony FX3 stock footage?

ProRes 422 or 422 HQ in a MOV, at the native size and frame rate. Every major stock site takes it.

Can I sell 120 fps slow motion from the FX3?

Yes, but deliver it at a normal playback rate. Shooting in S&Q mode records it already slowed down, so the file plays at 24 or 30 fps. A raw 120 fps file isn't on most sites' lists.

Does Stock Shredder read Sony XAVC files?

Yes. FX3 MP4 and FX6 MXF files both work, and it has a built-in Sony Look.

How long does it take to prepare 1,000 Sony clips?

By hand, about 33 hours at 2 minutes a clip. In our test on FX6 and FX3 footage, Stock Shredder shredded and exported 1,000 graded 4K ProRes clips in 3 hours 1 minute, not counting review.
