Best Websites to Sell Stock Footage

Every site pays differently for video. Here's what each one pays, what it asks of you, and how to sell on several at once.

The short version

  1. Start with Adobe Stock, Shutterstock and Pond5. All three are open to new contributors.
  2. Stay non-exclusive at first and upload the same clips to all three.
  3. Add Storyblocks for subscription income, and apply to iStock or Artlist once you have a portfolio.
  4. Short on time? Blackbox uploads to many sites for 15% of net sales.
  5. The real limit is prep: every site wants trimmed, graded, named, keyworded clips.

Adobe Stock.

Adobe pays 35% of the net price on video, a little more than its 33% on photos. It's non-exclusive, so the same clips can go everywhere else too. You can request a payout once you pass $25.

More: the Adobe Stock video workflow.

Shutterstock.

Video has its own six earnings levels, from 15% to 40% of what Shutterstock receives. You climb by video downloads, and every level resets to Level 1 on January 1. It's non-exclusive and pays monthly.

More: Shutterstock for video contributors.

Pond5.

Pond5 pays 40% on exclusive video and 30% on non-exclusive. You set your price, though Pond5 can adjust it under its tiered pricing. Payouts go out on the 15th once you pass $25. Exclusive Pond5 artists can still sell video on Shutterstock.

More: Pond5 upload rules and file names.

iStock and Getty Images.

You apply first, with 6 samples. Video samples go in as YouTube links. Royalties run from 15% to 45% depending on your agreement, and exclusive contributors get the higher rates. The exact video rates are on the rate card inside your contributor account.

Storyblocks.

Storyblocks is subscription only. Contributors share a part of subscription revenue, split by how often their clips are downloaded compared with everyone else's. Video is non-exclusive, and you can remove clips with 30 days' notice.

More: selling footage on Storyblocks.

Artlist (formerly Artgrid).

Artlist is curated. You apply with a portfolio, and review can take up to 3 months. Contributors share 30% of net subscription revenue in the footage pool, by their share of downloads, paid once a year. It's non-exclusive, so it can sit next to the open sites.

Blackbox.

Blackbox uploads your footage to Shutterstock, Pond5, Adobe Stock, iStock and several other sites under its own accounts. It's free to join and keeps 15% of net sales. You trade a cut for one upload instead of many.

Exclusive or not?

  • Non-exclusive puts every clip on several sites. More chances to sell, more upload work.
  • Exclusive pays a higher rate on one site, but only that site sells the clip.
  • Starting out, most contributors go non-exclusive. You learn which site sells your kind of footage before you commit.

Every site wants the same prep.

Whichever sites you pick, each clip has to be found, trimmed, graded, exported, named and keyworded first. At about 2 minutes a clip, 500 clips is 16+ hours before any upload.

What to know before you start.

Stock Shredder handles the repetitive parts. A few things stay with you.

  • You pick the sites. Read each site's terms, especially before going exclusive.
  • You review. It finds the usable shots. Your eye picks the ones that sell.
  • You upload. Keywords and upload sheets are ready, so each site's form goes fast.
  • One prep, every site. The same exported clips and keywords work on all of them, so adding a site costs minutes, not days.

When one site is enough.

  • You've signed an exclusive deal that pays more than spreading out would.
  • Your footage is a niche one curated site is built for.
  • You'd rather pay Blackbox's cut than upload to each site yourself.

Otherwise, more sites means more chances for every clip. The prep is the same either way, and that's the part worth handing off.

FAQ

Which stock site pays the most for video?

By published royalty rate, Pond5 pays 40% for exclusive video and 30% for non-exclusive, Shutterstock pays 15% to 40% by yearly level, and Adobe Stock pays 35%. What you earn per clip also depends on the price the buyer pays, so the rate alone doesn't decide it.

Can I sell the same clip on several sites?

Yes, unless you sign an exclusive deal. Adobe Stock, Shutterstock and Storyblocks are non-exclusive. Pond5 and iStock pay more if you go exclusive with them.

Which site is best for beginners?

Adobe Stock and Shutterstock have open sign-up and clear upload rules. Pond5 is open too. iStock and Artlist review an application first.

What is Blackbox?

A free service that uploads your footage to several stock sites for you, including Shutterstock, Pond5, Adobe Stock and iStock. It keeps 15% of net sales.

Do I need different files for each site?

Mostly no. One graded, high-quality export with a clean file name works on most sites, as long as it fits each site's length and size limits. Titles, keywords and the upload form differ by site.
