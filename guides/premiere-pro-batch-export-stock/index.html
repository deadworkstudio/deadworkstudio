Stock Shredder vs Manual Editing in Premiere Pro

Premiere is a great editor. Exporting 300 selects as 300 separate graded files is not what it was built for. Here's how to do it, and the faster way.

Premiere can do it. It just takes all night.

Most stock shooters already edit in Premiere Pro, so it's the first place they try. Drop the footage in, grade it, cut out the good moments, export. The trouble starts at "export": stock sites want every shot as its own file.

At about 2 minutes a clip, a 500-clip drive is 16+ hours. In Premiere, a good share of that is setting up exports one by one.

How to batch export stock clips in Premiere Pro.

  1. Grade once. Put your LUT or Lumetri grade on an adjustment layer over the whole timeline.
  2. Mark each shot. Set an in and out for the first select, 10 to 20 seconds.
  3. Queue it. Export with ProRes 422 HQ, audio off, and send it to Media Encoder instead of exporting now.
  4. Name it. Type the file name in the export dialog.
  5. Repeat. Move to the next shot and do steps 2 to 4 again.
  6. Render. Start the Media Encoder queue and let it run.

Some editors nest each shot into its own sequence and queue the sequences instead. Either way, every clip is its own setup. Adobe's forums are full of requests for a true "export each clip" option.

Sources: Adobe community feature request for batch export of individual clips. Checked November 2026.

Where the hours still go.

  • Finding the moments. You scrub every file to find usable shots.
  • Setting ranges. One in and out, or one sequence, per clip.
  • Naming. Typed by hand in each export.
  • Keywords. Not part of Premiere at all. That's another pass in a spreadsheet or the upload form.
  • Stock checks. Logos, frame rates and size are on you to catch.

The Stock Shredder way.

Point Stock Shredder at the raw footage on your Mac or Windows computer, before it ever hits a timeline.

  • It finds the moments. Usable shots are found and trimmed inside the length you set. No scrubbing.
  • It grades. Built-in Looks for ARRI, DJI, RED and Sony, or the same LUT you use in Premiere.
  • It exports every clip. Each its own ProRes file up to 8K, graded, log or both in one pass.
  • It checks. Logos, HDR, wrong frame rates and footage under 2K are flagged before you upload.
  • It names and keywords. Clean names, a folder per clip, and keywords ready to paste.

What to know before you start.

Stock Shredder handles the repetitive parts. A few things stay with you.

  • You review. It finds the usable shots. Your eye picks the ones that sell.
  • Premiere stays your editor. Use it for the clips that need real editing.
  • You upload. Names and keywords are ready to paste into each site.
  • It exports ProRes. The same format you'd pick in Premiere, and every site takes it.

When Premiere still makes sense.

  • Clips built from several shots, like a speed ramp, a composite or titles.
  • A few hero clips that need a custom grade.
  • You're exporting ten clips, not a drive.

For a full drive of single shots, every clip needs the same steps. That's the part worth handing off.

FAQ

Can Premiere Pro export each clip on a timeline as its own file?

Not in one click. The usual way is to set in and out points or make a sequence per shot, then queue each one to Adobe Media Encoder. It works, but every clip is its own setup.

What export settings should I use for stock footage in Premiere?

ProRes 422 or 422 HQ in a MOV, at the sequence's native size and frame rate, audio off. Every major stock site takes it.

Does Media Encoder make batch export faster?

It runs the renders in a queue while you keep working, which helps. You still set up each clip's range and name by hand.

Is Stock Shredder a Premiere Pro plugin?

No. It's its own app for Mac and Windows. You point it at raw footage and it finds, trims, grades, exports, names and keywords the clips. Premiere stays your editor for custom work.

How long does it take to prepare 1,000 stock clips?

By hand, about 33 hours at 2 minutes a clip. In our test, Stock Shredder shredded and exported 1,000 graded 4K ProRes clips in 3 hours 1 minute, not counting review.
