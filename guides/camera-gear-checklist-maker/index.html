How to use it.

  1. Pick the shoot. Each shoot type starts with the gear it usually needs. Cameras and shoot days set the counts for bodies, batteries and cards.
  2. Make it yours. Untick what you don't bring, change any count, and add your own items under each kit.
  3. Print or download. The printed page has a box for packing and a box for wrap. The CSV opens in any spreadsheet app.

Three things that stop gear going missing.

The full list, with what each item is for, is in our camera gear checklist for filmmakers.

  • Number the look-alikes. Label batteries, cards and drives 1, 2, 3, and check each number, not just the total.
  • One list per case. Short lists get checked. A three-page list gets skimmed at wrap.
  • Check before the lid closes. Once a case is shut and in the truck, nobody opens it again. Then do a two-minute check before you leave set.

FAQ

Is this checklist free to print?

Yes. Make your list, press Print, and you get a clean page with a box for when you pack and a box for when you wrap. The download is a CSV file that opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets.

How many camera batteries should I bring?

The maker starts at three per camera for a one-day shoot and four per camera for longer ones. Change the count to match how long your batteries last on your camera.

Why does the list have two boxes per item?

One for packing before the shoot and one for wrap. Wrap is when most gear gets left behind, so the second check matters most.

Does this save my list?

Only in your own browser, so it's there next time you open this page on the same device. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

Can I check the list with my phone instead of paper?

Yes, with Loadout on iPhone. Save each kit once, then pan the camera across the open case. It lists what it hasn't seen, and look-alike items wait for your tap.
