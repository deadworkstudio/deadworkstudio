Looking for an Xpiks Alternative for Stock Video?

Xpiks handled titles, keywords and uploads. For video, the slow part comes before that. Here's what to use now.

The short version

  1. Xpiks edited titles, keywords and descriptions, and uploaded over FTP.
  2. xpiks.com went to a domain-for-sale page in November 2026.
  3. For photos, pick another metadata tool.
  4. For video, the keywords are the last step. Trimming, grading and exporting take far longer.
  5. Stock Shredder does all of that plus names and keywords, in one pass.

1,000 clips took 3 hours 1 minute in our test, keywords included.

Xpiks fixed the last 10 minutes. Video needs the first 10 hours fixed.

For a lot of stock shooters, Xpiks was the tool that made metadata bearable. Load a batch, write keywords once, push them to every file, upload.

With video, metadata is the short part. Before a clip ever needs keywords, it has to be found, trimmed, graded and exported as its own file. At about 2 minutes a clip, 500 clips is 16+ hours of that.

So if you're replacing Xpiks for video, replace the whole chain, not just the keyword box.

What Xpiks did, fairly.

  • Metadata. Titles, descriptions and keywords written into your files.
  • Suggestions. Keyword suggestions from stock site searches or your own library.
  • Uploads. Sent files to stock sites over FTP, with saved logins.
  • Free and cross-platform. Mac, Windows and Linux, with open-source code.

Sources: Xpiks code on GitHub, AlternativeTo listing. xpiks.com checked November 12, 2026.

The Stock Shredder way.

Point Stock Shredder at a folder of raw footage on your Mac or Windows computer.

  • It finds and trims. Usable moments come out as clips inside the length you set.
  • It grades and exports. Each clip its own ProRes file, graded, log or both.
  • It names. Every shot gets a clear name and its own folder.
  • It keywords. Ready-to-paste keywords for every exported clip on Pro, Studio and the free trial. Copy one clip or all at once.

What to know before you start.

Stock Shredder handles the repetitive parts. A few things stay with you.

  • You review. It finds the usable shots. Your eye picks the ones that sell.
  • You upload. Keywords are ready to paste, so each site's form fills in seconds.
  • It's for video. Keep a photo tool for stills, and let Stock Shredder take the footage.
  • Check your format. Canon Cinema RAW Light and Nikon N-RAW aren't supported yet. Sony, DJI, ARRI, RED and Blackmagic files are.

When another tool makes more sense.

  • You mostly sell photos or vectors.
  • Your clips are already cut and graded, and you only need metadata.
  • You need FTP upload built in.

For raw footage that still needs everything, the keyword box is the easy part. That's why the whole chain is worth handing off.

FAQ

What did Xpiks do?

Xpiks was a free desktop app for Mac, Windows and Linux. It edited titles, descriptions and keywords in your files' metadata, suggested keywords, and uploaded files to stock sites over FTP.

Is Xpiks still available?

When we checked on November 12, 2026, xpiks.com redirected to a domain-for-sale page. Copies of its open-source code are still on GitHub, but there is no active download page we could find.

Is Stock Shredder a direct Xpiks replacement?

For video, it covers more: finding the moments, trimming, grading, exporting, naming and keywords. It does not upload over FTP and it does not work on photos or vectors.

How do I get keywords into Adobe Stock, Pond5 or Shutterstock?

On Pro, Studio and the free trial, Stock Shredder writes ready-to-paste keywords for every exported clip. One click copies them into each site's upload form.

What if I mostly shoot photos?

Then you want a photo metadata tool, not Stock Shredder. Stock Shredder is built for video clips only.
