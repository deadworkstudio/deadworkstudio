The short version

  1. A record of what you own: GearDex or Camera Shelf.
  2. A packing list to tick: ShootPrep (free), PhotoPack Pro or GigBag.
  3. A gear room many people borrow from: Sortly or Cheqroom.
  4. Checking a packed case before you leave: Loadout, which uses the iPhone camera instead of a list you tick.

Not ready for an app? Our camera gear checklist for filmmakers is a good place to start.

Three different jobs.

"Camera gear inventory app" covers apps that do quite different things. Before comparing features, decide which job you need done. Most people need the first and the third, and few need the second.

  • Knowing what you own. A list of every body, lens and accessory, with serial numbers, photos and what you paid. You update it when you buy or sell something.
  • Lending it out. Several people taking gear from one room, with check-out, check-in and who has what. This is where barcodes and QR codes earn their keep.
  • Getting it all home. Checking that each case is complete before it leaves the house and again at wrap. This is the moment gear actually goes missing.

Our guide to keeping track of camera equipment goes into all three records in more detail.

For checking the case before you leave.

Loadout

Loadout is our app, so take this one with that in mind. It does one job: checking that a packed case is complete. You save each case as a kit, then pan the iPhone across the open case. Items the camera recognizes are marked Seen, and anything it hasn't seen stays on the missing list. Only items you confirm with a tap count as packed, so look-alike batteries or a lens hidden under a cable wait for you.

  • Good at: the moment before you leave set. No tags or QR codes to stick on. Works offline. Rentals carry a return-by date, Wrap Day puts every kit into one check, and each finished check is saved with the date and time. Gear lists export as a PDF or spreadsheet.
  • Not built for: lending gear to a team, tracking what your gear is worth, or Android (coming soon). Each person checks on their own iPhone.

For knowing what you own.

Camera Shelf

A gear library for photographers and filmmakers on iPhone, iPad and Mac, with packing kits, a weight calculator and the value of what's in your bag. Recent versions add fast packing checklists that pack or unpack a whole subkit in one tap. Premium is a one-time $9.99 purchase that unlocks unlimited items and kits, PDF export and receipt storage, and adds NFC tag support. Its App Store privacy label says Data Not Collected.

  • Good for: one owner who wants a tidy record and kits without a subscription.
  • Keep in mind: it's a new app with few ratings so far, and packing is ticked by hand.

GearDex

A gear library for photographers, videographers and studios that also covers loans, shoot plans and packing manifests. It runs in the browser and has Android, Mac and Windows apps. Each item can get a QR label. The free plan holds up to 25 items. Paid plans are billed yearly, and the page shows more than one figure for them, so check the current price before you sign up.

  • Good for: people who want inventory and shoot planning in one place, or who work across Android and a computer.
  • Keep in mind: no native iPhone app is listed; on iPhone you'd use the web app.

For packing lists you tick.

GigBag

Reusable, resettable checklists for musicians, filmmakers and photographers, with templates per event and iCloud sync across your Apple devices. Free to download, with a monthly, yearly or lifetime upgrade.

PhotoPack Pro

A paid iPhone app ($2.99) for building a gear checklist per shoot or trip. It reminds you before the date if a list isn't complete, works offline and keeps data on the phone. Its privacy label says Data Not Collected.

ShootPrep

A free, very simple packing checklist for photographers, with starter lists for weddings, headshots and similar shoots. It works offline, needs no account, and lets you share a text copy of a list with an assistant.

Packaroo

A travel packing list app that also works for gear, with lists organized by bag or container and an option to hide packed items. The App Store shows its last update in 2023, so weigh that if you rely on it.

All four are a step up from a note on your phone. The catch is the same for each: a list you tick from memory is only as good as your attention at wrap. If you'd rather start with a list, our free camera gear checklist maker builds one by kit for your shoot type, and you can copy it into any of these apps.

For a gear room many people borrow from.

Sortly

General inventory software for businesses, with barcode and QR scanning on the phone, label printing, offline use and pick lists. It isn't made for camera gear specifically, but it handles any items. The free plan covers 100 items and one user. Paid plans add users and items and are billed monthly or yearly, with a first-year discount shown on the pricing page.

Cheqroom

Equipment check-out software for schools, studios and production companies. It tracks who has what, takes reservations, groups gear into kits, reads QR codes, barcodes and RFID, and works offline. Pricing is by quote, and every plan includes unlimited users.

If your problem is "who borrowed the C-stand," one of these is the right tool. If your problem is "is everything back in my own case," they're more system than you need.

How to choose.

  1. Name the moment gear goes missing for you. For most owner-operators it's wrap, not the gear room.
  2. Count the people. One person with their own kit needs a list and a check. Ten people sharing a room need check-out and tags.
  3. Check the platform. Several of these are Apple only. GearDex and Sortly cover Android.
  4. Try the free version on a real job. Pack one case for one shoot with it and see whether you'd keep using it.

Whatever you pick, the habit matters more than the app. Our two-minute gear check before you leave set works with any of them, or with none.

FAQ

What is the best camera gear inventory app?

It depends on the job. For a record of everything you own, a gear library like GearDex or Camera Shelf. For packing lists, ShootPrep, PhotoPack Pro or GigBag. For many people borrowing from one gear room, Sortly or Cheqroom. For checking that a packed case is complete before you leave, Loadout.

Is there a free camera gear inventory app?

Yes. ShootPrep is free, and Camera Shelf, Packaroo, GigBag, GearDex and Sortly all have free versions with limits. Loadout is free for 14 days with no card.

Do I need barcodes or QR codes to track camera gear?

Not for one owner's kit. Tags help when lots of people check gear in and out of one room, which is what Sortly and Cheqroom are built for. Loadout doesn't use tags at all: it checks the open case with the iPhone camera.

Can an app tell me if something is missing from my camera bag?

Most apps here give you a checklist to tick by hand. Loadout uses the iPhone camera: pan across the open case and anything it hasn't seen stays on the missing list. Items it matches are marked Seen, and only items you confirm count as packed.

Which app should a team use?

If several people share one gear room and need check-in and check-out, look at Sortly or Cheqroom. Loadout is built for one person checking their own kits on their own iPhone.
