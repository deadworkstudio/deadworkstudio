How to Get Footage Onto Pond5 Faster

Pond5 takes more formats than most sites and is stricter about file names. Here's what it wants, and how to get a full drive ready fast.

The short version

  1. Cut clips to 5 to 60 seconds, one shot each.
  2. MOV or MP4. Camera native or ProRes first.
  3. File names: letters, numbers, underscores. No spaces, dashes or accents.
  4. Up to 50 keywords. 10 to 20 good ones work best.
  5. Remove the audio unless it matters.

Stock Shredder trims, grades, exports, names and keywords the whole drive in one pass. 1,000 clips took 3 hours 1 minute in our test.

Pond5 is open. Getting clips ready isn't.

Pond5 takes almost anything a camera records, up to 8K. The slow part is the same as everywhere else: trim, grade, export, name and keyword, one clip at a time.

At about 2 minutes a clip, a 500-clip drive is 16+ hours at the desk. Pond5 adds one more thing to get right on every file: the name.

What's different about Pond5.

  • File names count. Pond5 doesn't allow spaces, dashes, accents or special characters. A name like sunset beach-final.mov has to change. Underscores are the safe choice.
  • Pricing is tiered for video. The price you enter is one of several factors. You can also let Pond5 set it.
  • Payout. 40% for exclusive video, 30% for non-exclusive, paid on the 15th once you pass $25.
  • 48 fps works. Unlike Adobe Stock, Pond5 lists 47.95, 47.96 and 48.
  • One shot per file. Split montages into single clips.

Selling on Adobe Stock and Shutterstock too? The side-by-side is in How to Prepare Footage for Adobe Stock, Pond5 and Shutterstock.

The by-hand way.

  1. Scrub each file and set an in and out.
  2. Grade log to a light Rec 709.
  3. Export each clip as its own file, audio off.
  4. Rename it with no spaces, dashes or accents.
  5. Write a title and 10 to 20 strong keywords.
  6. Set a price, or let Pond5 set it.

Our average is about 2 minutes per clip, before pricing.

The Stock Shredder way.

Point Stock Shredder at a folder of raw footage on your Mac or Windows computer.

  • It finds the moments. Usable shots are found and trimmed inside the length you set.
  • It checks. Logos, HDR, wrong frame rates and footage under 2K are flagged before you upload.

  • It exports. Each clip its own ProRes file up to 8K, graded, log or both in one pass.
  • It names cleanly. Each clip gets a folder named for the shot and a file like graded_001.mov. No spaces, no dashes.
  • It keywords. Keywords ready to paste into the Pond5 upload form.

What to know before you start.

Stock Shredder handles the repetitive parts. A few things stay with you.

  • You review. It finds the usable shots. Your eye picks the ones worth sending to Pond5.
  • You set the price. Pricing is your call on Pond5. Everything before it is done.
  • You upload. Names and keywords are ready, so the Pond5 form goes fast.
  • It exports ProRes. High on Pond5's list, and short clips stay well under the size caps.

When by hand still makes sense.

  • You want to upload the untouched camera file, which Pond5 lists first.
  • A handful of hero clips that each need a custom edit.

For a full drive, every clip needs the same steps. That's the part worth handing off.

FAQ

What file names does Pond5 accept?

Plain letters, numbers and underscores. Pond5's own guide says no special characters, no accents, and no spaces or dashes in file names.

How big can a Pond5 video file be?

Up to 3 GB for HD and vertical, 5 GB for 4K to 6K, and 6 GB for 8K. R3D zipped with a MOV can be up to 6 GB.

How many keywords can I add on Pond5?

Up to 50 per file. Pond5 says 10 to 20 good keywords work best.

Do I set my own price for video on Pond5?

Pond5 uses tiered pricing for video. The price you enter is one of several factors in the final list price, or you can let Pond5 set it.

How much does Pond5 pay for video?

Pond5's payout page lists a 40% share for exclusive video contributors and 30% for non-exclusive. Payouts go out on the 15th with a $25 minimum.

Should I keep the audio on Pond5 clips?

Usually not. Pond5 prefers audio removed, unless it's editorial or the sound is key to the clip.
