The Film Production Wrap Checklist for Camera Gear

Wrap is where gear gets left behind. Here's the order to wrap in, what to check in each case, and a free printable sheet.

The short version

  1. Pick one staging area. Every piece of gear comes back there.
  2. Give each case an owner who signs it off.
  3. Sweep the places gear hides: stands, carts, sockets, readers.
  4. Count look-alikes by number, not by total.
  5. Check each case open, then close it, then load it.

Why wrap needs its own checklist.

Your packing list tells you what to bring. Wrap is a different job: the gear has been spread across a set all day, people are tired, and the truck has a call time. Nothing on a packing list tells you to look behind the monitor cart for a charger.

A good wrap checklist follows the gear, not the kit list. It sends you to the places things end up during a day, then has you prove each case is complete before it closes. If you don't have a kit list yet, start with our camera gear checklist for filmmakers.

The order to wrap in.

  1. Call it. When wrap is called, the camera team stops building and starts collecting. Nothing new gets pulled out.
  2. Download first. Cards come out of cameras and go to the data station before anything is torn down. Mark full cards the way your team agreed.
  3. Strip the cameras. Lenses off and capped, monitors and wireless off the rig, batteries off. Each piece goes straight to its case, not to a pile.
  4. Sweep the set. Walk every position the camera used. Tripods, sliders, stands, carts, apple boxes, wall sockets.
  5. Check each case open. One owner, one case, one list. Count, confirm, then close.
  6. Load and count cases. The number of cases on the truck matches the number on your list.
  7. Last look. One person walks the location with a light after the truck is loaded.

Where gear hides at wrap.

Most lost gear isn't lost inside a case. It's somewhere it was used.

  • On the tripod. Quick release plates and baseplates stay on the head.
  • In the wall. Battery chargers, laptop chargers and power strips.
  • In the reader. The last card of the day, still in the card reader or the laptop.
  • On stands and carts. Batteries, lens caps, small monitors, articulating arms.
  • On people. Lav transmitters on talent, slates and markers in pockets, a lens in a jacket.
  • Under things. Sandbags, apple boxes and dark corners of the stage.

The case-by-case check.

Run this for every case before it closes. It's also the layout of the printable sheet.

CaseCheck
CameraBody, body cap, top handle, cage, plates, follow focus, matte box
LensesEvery lens by focal length, front and rear caps, filters back in their pouches
PowerBatteries by number, chargers and their cables, D-tap and USB cables
MediaCards by number, readers, drives, card wallet with full and empty marked
MonitoringMonitors, sunhoods, wireless transmitter and receiver as a pair, SDI and HDMI cables by count
SoundRecorder, mics, windscreens, lav kits as pairs, XLR cables by count, headphones
Grip and lightingLights and power supplies, stands by count, clamps by count, diffusion
ToolsHex keys, multitool, flashlight, tape, markers, rain cover

Count look-alikes by number.

Identical items are the classic wrap loss. You own four batteries, three come home, and the case looks full because there's no empty slot to notice. Label each battery, card and drive with a number and check each number. The same goes for things that come in pairs, like wireless video and lav kits.

The Loadout way.

Paper works, but it's the step people skip in a dark room at the end of a long day. Loadout runs the same case check with your iPhone camera.

  1. One kit per case. Save each case once: the camera package, the grip package, the sound bag.
  2. Wrap Day. Put every kit into one wrap check and scan each one before you call wrap.
  3. Pan the open case. Each item the camera spots is marked Seen. Seen is not packed.
  4. Confirm. Look-alikes, like two identical batteries, each wait for your tap. Then tap All packed.
  5. Leave with a record. Anything not seen stays on the missing list. The finished check is saved with the date and time.

It works with no signal, warns you when the case is too dark and turns on the light with one tap. You can also turn on a reminder for when your phone leaves the location.

What to know before you start.

Loadout handles the counting. A few things stay with you.

  • You confirm. Only items you confirm count as packed, so a false match never closes a case.
  • The sweep is still yours. Loadout checks what's in the case. Walking the set for what isn't is still the job of the last person out.
  • Buried items need a look. Anything under other gear stays on the list until you see it, or check it by hand with one tap.
  • iPhone for now. Android is coming soon. Each person checks on their own phone.

When paper makes more sense.

If it's one bag and one person, a printed sheet in the lid may be all you need. The order above still applies: download first, sweep the set, count look-alikes by number, close the case last. Before you leave, run our two-minute check before leaving set.

FAQ

What is a wrap checklist?

A list you run at the end of a shoot day to make sure every piece of gear is back in its case before the cases close and get loaded. For the camera team it covers bodies, lenses, batteries, media, monitoring, cables and the small things that live outside a case.

Who runs the camera wrap?

On larger shoots the 2nd AC usually leads it with the 1st AC. On small shoots it's whoever owns or rented the gear. What matters is that one person owns each case and signs it off.

What gets left behind most at wrap?

In our experience it's the small things that live outside a case during the day: quick release plates on tripods, chargers in wall sockets, cards in readers, lens caps and batteries on stands. Check those spots on purpose.

Should I check gear at wrap if I checked it when I packed?

Yes. The pack check proves you left with everything. Only the wrap check proves you came back with it.

Can I run the wrap check with my phone?

Yes, with Loadout on iPhone. Put each case in a kit once, then pan the camera across the open case. Items it hasn't seen stay on the missing list, and look-alikes wait for your tap. It works with no signal.
