How to Keep Track of Camera Equipment

Three questions keep gear from going missing: what you own, where it is, and when you last saw it. Here's a simple answer for each.

The short version

  1. What you own: one master list with serial numbers, photos and prices.
  2. Where it is: a short list per case, so every item has a home.
  3. When you last saw it: a dated check every time a case leaves and comes back.
  4. Keep rentals in their own case list with the return date on it.

Three questions, three records.

Most gear tracking stops at a spreadsheet of everything you own. That answers "what do I have?" but not "where is it?" or "did it come back from Tuesday's shoot?" Those last two are where gear actually goes missing.

1. What you own: the master list.

One row per item. For anything you'd file a claim on, add the details you can't get back later.

  • Item and model. "Lens, 35mm prime" is fine for you. The model name is what an insurer or buyer wants.
  • Serial number. Take it off the item, not the box. Photograph it too.
  • Photo. One clear picture of each body and lens, with any marks or engraving you'd use to identify it.
  • Price and date. What you paid and when, with the receipt saved somewhere you won't lose it.
  • Which case. The name of the case it lives in. This links the master list to the next record.

A spreadsheet works well here. If insurance is the reason you're doing this, check your own policy for what it asks for.

2. Where it lives: one list per case.

Give every item a home and write the home down. A case list is short, so it actually gets checked: one camera case, one lens case, one power bag, one sound bag. If you're not sure what goes where, the camera gear checklist maker builds lists by kit for your shoot type.

  • Same place every time. If the 50mm always sits in the second slot, an empty slot tells you something.
  • Number the look-alikes. Batteries, cards and drives get a number each, so "four batteries" becomes 1, 2, 3 and 4.
  • Pairs stay pairs. Wireless video and lav kits are listed as a transmitter and a receiver, not one item.
  • Small things count. Plates, caps and chargers go on the list. They're what gets left behind.

3. When you last saw it: the dated check.

This is the record most people skip, and the one that tells you where to look. If the lens case was checked complete on Monday's wrap and a lens is missing on Thursday, you know it went missing after Monday.

  1. Check when the case leaves. Open the case and tick its list before it goes in the car.
  2. Check at wrap. Same list, before the case closes on set. Our film production wrap checklist has the order to do it in.
  3. Write the date. A ticked list with no date doesn't tell you much a week later.

Rented gear needs its own list.

Rentals get mixed in with your own gear on set and then go home in the wrong bag. Keep them separate.

  • Own case list. Everything from one rental order goes on one list, even if it rides in your cases.
  • Return date on top. Write the return-by date where you'll see it at wrap.
  • Check out and check in. Tick the list when you pick it up and again before you hand it back.

FAQ

What's the easiest way to keep track of camera equipment?

Split it into three records: a list of everything you own, a list per case of what lives in it, and a dated check every time a case leaves and comes back. Most people only keep the first one.

Should I record serial numbers?

Yes. Write down the serial number, a photo and what you paid for every body, lens and expensive accessory. You'll want it for insurance, a theft report or selling the gear later. Check your own policy for what your insurer asks for.

Do I need QR codes or asset tags?

Not for a small kit. Tags help a rental house with hundreds of items moving through a counter. For one owner or a small team, a list per case and a check at wrap does most of the work.

How do I keep track of rented gear?

Keep rentals in their own kit, with the return date written on it. Check that kit at wrap like your own gear, so nothing rented ends up in your bag or left on set.

Can I keep track of camera gear with my phone?

Yes, with Loadout on iPhone. Save each case as a kit, then pan the camera across the open case to see what's missing. Each finished check is saved with the date and time, and rentals carry a return-by date.
