01 · Verified

Green means safe.
Nothing else does.

Handoff doesn't stop at "copy finished". It reads every file back from every hard drive, matches it to the card, and only then gives you one of four answers.

  • Green Safe.

    Every clip verified on the number of copies your rule asks for. You can format the card.

  • Amber Not safe yet.

    What was copied is good, but more verified copies are needed. Add one and it turns green.

  • Red Don't format.

    It failed. It names the hard drive and the clips. Re-copy only those.

  • Grey Interrupted.

    The Mac lost power, slept or quit mid-copy. Resume and it picks up from what's verified.

The copy rule

Green needs every clip verified on 2 separate hard drives. Change it from 1 to 5 in Settings. Two folders on the same physical disk only count once.

Backing up or transferring from a hard drive? If Handoff confirms the original is a hard drive, it counts as one copy. A card never does. Green then says Original + 1 copy. Keep the original. It won’t tell you to format it.

02 · No more second-guessing

Every card has a history.

Copying leaves you a folder of files. Handoff leaves proof: which files matched, on which hard drives, and when. Nobody has to remember, and nobody has to open the footage to check.

On file for every card

  • Production label and shoot day
  • Card name, date and time
  • Source and every hard drive it went to
  • Clips matched on each hard drive
  • The result, and what went wrong if it failed

Left on every hard drive

  • A PDF custody report with every file's xxHash64. It only says safe when the card was green.
  • MHL checksum files, legacy and ASC MHL, for post to check delivery
  • A CSV of the same list

Find it later

  • Search by card, clip or label
  • Filter by day, or by not safe
  • Wrap report as PDF or CSV
  • Footage catalog as CSV
  • Kept until you delete it
