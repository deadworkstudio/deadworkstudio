You can't pick the winners. You can pick the field.

Nobody knows in advance which clip will sell, and anyone promising sales per clip is guessing. What the sites do publish is what their buyers ask for. Shoot toward that list, get a lot of it online, and let sales tell you the rest.

Here's what Adobe Stock and Shutterstock tell contributors, from their own pages.

Evergreen themes.

  • Food. Cooking, eating, ingredients, kitchens.
  • Families. Adobe calls out multigenerational and multiracial family groups.
  • Health and wellness. Exercise, rest, care, everyday health.
  • Nature and wildlife. Landscapes, animals, weather. Adobe also mentions a new drone perspective.
  • Business. Teamwork, digital transformation and the future of work.
  • Real people. Adobe asks for people with disabilities shown in a sensitive, empowering and realistic way. Recognizable people need a model release.

Source: Adobe Stock, create what's in demand. Checked November 2026.

Shutterstock's current briefs.

Shutterstock posts contributor briefs. The ones that matter most for video:

  • Vertical video. Shutterstock says customers are increasingly searching for it.
  • Video plates. Clean background scenes in a wide range of settings, for editors to build on.
  • Homes. Exteriors, with a valid property release.
  • People and moments. Working families, sports fans, seniors, babies, emotions.
  • Seasons and holidays. Spring, summer, winter, New Year resolutions, Mother's Day, Eid, Diwali, Lunar New Year.
  • Industry and technology. Skilled work, video games and more.

Source: Shutterstock contributor content briefs. Briefs change often, so check the page before a shoot. Checked November 2026.

The look buyers want.

  • Real, not perfect. Adobe asks for natural, genuine moments and places, and warns against footage that looks posed or too perfect.
  • Timeless styling. Wardrobe and props modern but not too trendy.
  • Products out of focus. Identifiable devices, toys and accessories shouldn't be the subject. Adobe says not to always make devices the focus.
  • Sharp and clean. In focus at 100%, no heavy noise, good light.

Source: Adobe Stock, smart submission strategies. Checked November 2026.

Clips that miss these often come back rejected. See the stock footage requirements, side by side before you shoot.

Timing and crowded subjects.

  • Upload seasonal clips early. Adobe suggests about three months before buyers start looking, and two to three months before a holiday or industry event.
  • Plan holiday shoots far ahead. Adobe says several months to a year.
  • Know what's crowded. Adobe names lifestyle and business shots, flowers, pets and sunsets as saturated. Buyers still want fresh takes on best-selling themes, so bring a new angle.

From shoot to upload, by hand.

  1. Scrub each file for the usable moments.
  2. Trim each one to a stock length.
  3. Grade log footage to a clean Rec 709.
  4. Export each clip as its own file.
  5. Rename it so you can find it again.
  6. Write a title and keywords buyers would search.

Our average is about 2 minutes per clip. Shooting more of what sells only helps if you can get it online.

Point Stock Shredder at a folder of raw footage on your Mac or Windows computer.

What to know before you start.

Stock Shredder handles the repetitive parts. A few things stay with you.

  • You choose what to shoot. Demand decides sales more than any tool.
  • You get the releases. People and private property need signed releases for commercial use.
  • You review. It finds the usable shots. Your eye picks the ones that fit a brief.
  • More of each shoot goes online. With prep handled, a brief-driven shoot becomes a full batch of clips, not a few.

When by hand still makes sense.

  • You shot a handful of hero clips for one brief.
  • Every clip needs a custom edit.

For full cards and drives, the prep decides how much of what you shoot ever earns. That's the part worth handing off.
