The short version

  1. Cut clips to 5 to 60 seconds, under 10 GB.
  2. MOV or MP4, ProRes or H.264. Skip H.265 for now.
  3. Grade to Rec 709. 48 fps is fine here.
  4. Write the description as a sentence, then 7 to 50 keywords.
  5. No trademarks in keywords unless the clip is editorial.

The specs take a minute. The metadata takes all day.

Shutterstock's file rules are some of the loosest: up to 10 GB a clip, 48 fps welcome. The slow part is everything around the file. Trim, grade, export, then a description and up to 50 keywords for every single clip.

At about 2 minutes a clip, a 500-clip drive is 16+ hours of work, and a big share of it is typing.

Keywords and descriptions that pass.

  • Description first. One plain sentence: what's in the shot, where, and when. "Aerial view of the Manhattan skyline at sunset over the Hudson River" beats "manhattan skyline sunset aerial river".
  • Keywords next. 7 to 50, each word once. Start with what a buyer would type.
  • Leave out the camera. Camera and lens models don't belong in keywords.
  • Commercial or editorial. A visible logo, brand or recognizable person without a release makes it editorial. Brand names are only allowed in editorial keywords.

The by-hand way.

  1. Scrub each file and set an in and out.
  2. Grade log to Rec 709.
  3. Export each clip as its own file, audio off.
  4. Name the file and its folder.
  5. Write a sentence description.
  6. Type 7 to 50 keywords, checking for repeats and brands.

Our average is about 2 minutes per clip, every clip.

Stock Shredder handles the repetitive parts. A few things stay with you.

  • You choose editorial or commercial. Flagged logos make that call quick.

When by hand still makes sense.

  • A few editorial clips that need a dated, place-specific caption.
  • Clips built from several shots, like a speed ramp.

For a full drive, every clip needs the same steps. That's the part worth handing off.
